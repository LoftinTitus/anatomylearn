### Anatomy
The largest solid organ, sitting in the right upper quadrant under the diaphragm and protected by the lower right ribs. It is divided functionally into eight **Couinaud segments**, each with its own portal venous and hepatic arterial supply and biliary drainage — the basis for surgical resection.

It has a **dual blood supply**: about 75% from the **portal vein** (nutrient-rich blood from the gut) and 25% from the **hepatic artery** (oxygenated blood). Blood drains via the hepatic veins into the inferior vena cava.

### Physiology
- **Metabolism:** stores glucose as glycogen and releases it between meals; makes glucose from other substrates (gluconeogenesis).
- **Synthesis:** albumin, most clotting factors, and cholesterol.
- **Detoxification:** metabolises drugs and converts ammonia into urea.
- **Bile:** produced by hepatocytes, stored in the gallbladder, and used to emulsify fats.

### Pathophysiology
- **Steatosis (fatty liver):** fat accumulates in hepatocytes, commonly from metabolic syndrome or alcohol, and can progress to inflammation and fibrosis.
- **Cirrhosis:** chronic injury replaces normal tissue with fibrosis and nodules. This raises resistance to portal flow (**portal hypertension**: varices, ascites, splenomegaly) and reduces synthetic function (low albumin, prolonged clotting).

### On CT
Normally brighter than the spleen on unenhanced CT. A fatty liver appears darker than the spleen.
