### Anatomy
Lies in the left upper quadrant, behind the stomach and beneath ribs 9–11. It is supplied by the **splenic artery**, a branch of the coeliac trunk, and drained by the **splenic vein**, which joins the superior mesenteric vein to form the portal vein.

### Physiology
- **Red pulp:** filters the blood and removes old or damaged red cells.
- **White pulp:** lymphoid tissue that mounts immune responses, especially against encapsulated bacteria.

### Pathophysiology
- **Splenomegaly:** enlargement, for example from portal hypertension, infection (such as mononucleosis) or blood cancers.
- **Rupture:** the most commonly injured organ in blunt abdominal trauma.
- **Asplenia:** after splenectomy, patients are at higher risk of overwhelming infection by encapsulated organisms, so vaccination is recommended.

### On CT
In the arterial phase, enhancement can look mottled (a normal finding). It becomes uniform in the portal venous phase.
