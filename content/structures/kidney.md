### Anatomy
Paired, bean-shaped organs in the retroperitoneum at roughly the T12–L3 level. The right kidney usually sits slightly lower because the liver is above it. Each has an outer **cortex**, an inner **medulla** arranged in pyramids, and a **renal pelvis** that drains urine into the ureter. The renal artery, vein and pelvis enter at the **hilum**.

### Physiology
- **Filtration:** each kidney contains roughly a million **nephrons**. The glomerulus filters plasma, and the tubules reabsorb what the body needs.
- **Fluid and electrolyte balance:** regulates sodium, potassium, water and acid–base status.
- **Blood pressure:** the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system.
- **Hormones:** erythropoietin (red cell production) and activation of vitamin D.

### Pathophysiology
- **Acute kidney injury:** a sudden fall in filtration, classified as pre-renal (low perfusion), intrinsic (damage to the kidney itself), or post-renal (obstruction).
- **Chronic kidney disease:** progressive nephron loss, most often from diabetes or hypertension.
- **Simple cysts:** common, usually harmless fluid-filled lesions. This model segments them separately.

### On CT
After contrast, the cortex enhances before the medulla (corticomedullary phase). Later, contrast is excreted into the collecting system.
