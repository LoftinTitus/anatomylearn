### Anatomy
A retroperitoneal gland lying across the posterior abdomen at about L1–L2. The **head** sits in the C-shaped curve of the duodenum, and the **body** and **tail** extend toward the spleen. The main pancreatic duct joins the common bile duct and drains into the duodenum at the ampulla of Vater.

### Physiology
- **Exocrine (about 98% of the gland):** acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases), and duct cells secrete bicarbonate to neutralise stomach acid.
- **Endocrine:** the islets of Langerhans release **insulin** (beta cells) and **glucagon** (alpha cells) to regulate blood glucose.

### Pathophysiology
- **Acute pancreatitis:** enzymes activate inside the gland and digest it, most commonly due to gallstones or alcohol.
- **Pancreatic adenocarcinoma:** often presents late. A tumour in the head can block the bile duct and cause painless jaundice.
- **Diabetes mellitus:** type 1 involves autoimmune destruction of beta cells.

### On CT
Lobulated and fatty with age. Pancreatitis appears as swelling and stranding of the surrounding fat.
